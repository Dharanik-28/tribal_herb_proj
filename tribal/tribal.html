Tribal Areas

Explore Araku, Chintapalli and Paderu tribal regions and their medicinal plant heritage.

🏔️ Araku Valley

Araku Valley is a hilly region of the Eastern Ghats known for forests, coffee plantations, biodiversity and tribal communities. The region has a rich variety of trees, herbs and other plants.

🌿 Plants found / cultivated in the region

  • Turmeric (Curcuma longa)
  • Ginger (Zingiber officinale)
  • Amla (Phyllanthus emblica)
  • Tulsi (Ocimum tenuiflorum)
  • Neem (Azadirachta indica)

🍃 Traditional Plant Uses

Local communities have traditional knowledge about using different plants, fruits, roots and leaves for food, household purposes and traditional herbal practices.

🌳 Chintapalli

Chintapalli is surrounded by forested landscapes of the Eastern Ghats. The area is associated with diverse vegetation and traditional knowledge of forest resources.

🌿 Plants found / cultivated in the region

  • Turmeric (Curcuma longa)
  • Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)
  • Amla (Phyllanthus emblica)
  • Aloe Vera (Aloe vera)
  • Neem (Azadirachta indica)

🍃 Traditional Plant Uses

Forest resources are traditionally used for food, household products and herbal practices. Knowledge about plants is passed between generations within local communities.

🌿 Paderu

Paderu is a prominent tribal and forest region in the Alluri Sitharama Raju district of Andhra Pradesh. Its surrounding landscapes contain forests, agricultural areas and diverse plant resources.

🌿 Plants found / cultivated in the region

  • Tulsi (Ocimum tenuiflorum)
  • Turmeric (Curcuma longa)
  • Ginger (Zingiber officinale)
  • Amla (Phyllanthus emblica)
  • Neem (Azadirachta indica)

🍃 Traditional Plant Uses

Local plant knowledge includes traditional uses of leaves, fruits, roots and rhizomes. Sustainable cultivation and collection can also support local livelihoods.

🌍 Sustainable Plant Resources

Sustainable cultivation and responsible collection of plants are important for protecting biodiversity. Avoiding excessive harvesting helps maintain natural plant populations and supports future generations.
